BABCOCK UNIVERSITY LOST & FOUND SYSTEM (FINDIT)
Name of Project: FindIt
1. OVERVIEW
This digital solution will enable both students and university members to report and manage lost items and also to find lost property.
Students can report lost items including relevant information and photographs if possible. University members will then be able to document found items, and analyse to see whether links can be made between lost and found items.

Further, communication between students and university members can be enhanced via the system.
Whilst university members are still responsible for securely storing found items and confirming ownership, they can then release them to the correct owner. Students may then collect items from the FindIt office having presented suitable identification and evidence of ownership.
The FindIt System is designed to centralise and enhance the lost and found procedure.

Background and Context
Students commonly use their department’s WhatsApp group and/or ask those present in an area to try and locate lost items for them.
Although these methods can be effective in disseminating information regarding lost property, they lack a centralised system for both reporting and analysing information.

Following an item being found, it is documented and stored in the FindIt office. Yet there is currently no method in place to analyse and link this with information regarding lost items. As a result, it can be hard to ascertain, and subsequently communicate with, the correct owner of an item.
Whilst the university had previously had a Lost & Found system, this was sadly stopped some years ago. Unfortunately the exact reason(s) for this is/are unknown.
Such findings identified the necessity for a more structured system which can link students’ lost property reports with university members’ found items.
Product Vision
To develop a centralised system so that students can report lost property and university members can analyse information in order to ascertain and return items to their correct owners.


2. PROBLEM STATEMENT
Students generally use WhatsApp groups, ask those in an area and/or search for lost items.
Unfortunately, however, this is not a structured procedure. Often students do not then receive much or any subsequent analysis or action in relation to their report.
Moreover, university members struggle to analyse and ascertain the correct owner of found items. Although they can document and store found items in the FindIt office, there is no centralised method of analysing them to identify correct owners.
Therefore, it can be more challenging to both analyse students’ lost property reports and to return found items.
FindIt aims to overcome these issues and provide a centralised system so that students can report lost property and university members can document found items. Members can then analyse the information and communicate with potential owners in order to return items.


3. TARGET USERS
The FindIt system is aimed at two key user types:
Students
Students can report lost items and give relevant information regarding them. They will also be able to analyse their reports and receive communications in relation to them.
University Members
Members can document found items. They will be able to analyse both lost and found items and analyse links between them. They can then communicate with potential owners. In addition, they are responsible for analysing evidence of ownership and returning items to the correct owner.
Research
We carried out face-to-face interviews with 10 people (7 students and 3 university members).
Students were questioned about their lost property experiences.
University members were questioned about how they receive, document and return found items.
From these we learnt more about the current procedure, recognised issues and analysed aspects that could be improved.
Research Findings
Students commonly:
1. Report lost items by posting to their department’s WhatsApp group and/or by asking those in the local area.
2. Frequently do not receive much or any subsequent analysis or action in relation to their report.
University members:
3. Can document found items.
4. Lack a formal procedure to analyse and communicate with correct owners of found items.
5. Store found items in the FindIt office.
Prior to collection, individuals must present suitable identification and evidence of ownership.


. USER STORIES
Below are the functions which students and university members should be able to carry out using FindIt.
As a student, I would like to set up an account utilising my university information so that I can access the system.
As a student, I would like to securely sign in to and out of my account.
As a student, I would like to report lost items and give relevant information including the item’s name, type, date and location so that university members can analyse it.
As a student, I would like to add photographs of my lost item in order to further facilitate its analysis.
As a student, I would like to analyse my report so that I can ascertain whether or not action has been taken in relation to it.
As a student, I would like to receive communications regarding developments in relation to my report so that I am aware of relevant information.
As a university member, I would like to document found items so that there is an appropriate record of them.
As a university member, I would like to analyse and manage lost and found items.
As a university member, I would like to analyse links between lost and found items so that I can recognise potential matches.
As a university member, I would like to receive communications when students report lost items so that I can promptly analyse this information.

5. FUNCTIONAL AND NON-FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS
Functional Requirements
A. Registration and Sign-In
- Enable students to set up an account with their UMIS information.
- Enable users to securely sign in and sign out.
- Enable users to sign out.
- Only allow access to university members’ functionality to authorised university members.
  
B. Reporting Lost Items
- Enable students to input relevant information relating to lost items such as name, type, date and location.
- Enable students to add photographs of lost items where applicable.
- Enable the system to store students’ reports.
- Enable students to analyse their reports.

C. Documenting Found Items
- Enable university members to document found items.
- Enable university members to input relevant information regarding found items.
- Enable university members to add photographs of found items where appropriate.
- Enable the system to store documentation of found items and for university members to edit this where appropriate.
  
D. Analysing and Managing
- Enable university members to analyse lost and found items.
- Enable university members to analyse links between lost and found items.
- Enable the system to recognise and display potential links to facilitate university members in recognising correct owners.
- Enable university members to edit analyses of items.
  
E. Communications
- Enable the system to communicate with university members when students report lost items.
- Enable the system to communicate with students regarding developments in relation to their reports.
- Enable the system to communicate with university members when students make a claim for a found item.
  
F. Analysing Evidence of Ownership
- Enable university members to analyse evidence of someone’s identification and ownership.
- Require students to show identification and evidence of ownership prior to them collecting an item.
- Enable university members to recognise when an item has been successfully returned.
  
Non-functional Requirements
The system should:
- Be secure – ensuring users’ accounts and information are safeguarded through secure sign-in functionality and controlled access.
- Have good usability – so that it is easy for students and university members to utilise.
- Perform well – ensuring that pages can be loaded and information displayed in a reasonable time frame. Users should also be able to easily search for and analyse items.
- Be reliable – so that it can be accessed and used by students and university members at times when this is required (again, assuming there is internet access).
- Be compatible – so that it can be accessed via widely used web browsers and also on mobile and computer devices.
- Protect users’ information – ensuring that only authorised individuals can access this.
- Be easily amendable and improvable in the future.


6. MINIMUM VIABLE PRODUCT (MVP)
The MVP for FindIt will centre on core functions which enable students to report lost items and for university members to document, analyse and return found items.
These will include:
- Registration and sign-in functionality utilising university information.
- A student and university member dashboard.
- Reporting of lost items.
- Documentation of found items by university members.
- Entry of relevant information for items (name, type, date and location).
- Photograph addition functionality for both lost and found items.
- Analysis functionality for items.
- Basic linking functionality to enable university members to recognise potential matches.
- Communication functionality to inform university members of newly reported lost items and students of developments in relation to their reports.
- Student analysis functionality for reports.
- Evidence of identification and ownership analysis functionality so that university members can return items to the correct owner.
- University member functionality to recognise when an item has been returned.
Core functionalities will be emphasised in the MVP. Further functionalities may then be added in later stages.


7. ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA
FindIt will be deemed to function once:
- Registration – Students are able to set up an account using appropriate university information.
- Sign-in – Users can securely sign in and sign out.
- Reporting Lost Items – Students can effectively report lost items and input relevant information.
- Photograph Addition – Students and/or university members can add photographs to reports.
- Documentation of Found Items – Authorised university members can successfully document found items.
- Item Analysis and Management – University members can analyse and edit information relating to items.
- Item Linking – The system can recognise and display potential links between lost and found items.
- Communications – University members can receive communications regarding newly reported lost items. Students can also receive communications regarding developments in relation to their reports.
- Report Analysis – Students can analyse the current status of their reports.
- Evidence of Identification and Ownership Analysis – University members can analyse evidence of someone’s identification and ownership so that they can return items to the correct owner.
- Item Return Status Analysis – University members can recognise when an item has been successfully returned.
- Access Control – Unauthorised users are unable to gain access to university members’ functionality.


8. SCOPE, PRIORITIES AND FURTHER QUERIES
Scope
The MVP will centre on enabling students to report lost items and for university members to document, analyse and return found items.
Within the project:
- Registration and sign-in functionality will be provided.
- Lost item reporting functionality will be provided.
- Found item documentation functionality will be provided.
- Photograph addition functionality will be provided.
- Information entry functionality (for example, item name, type, date and location) will be provided.
- Item analysis and linking functionality will be provided.
- Communications functionality will be provided.
- Evidence of identification and ownership analysis functionality will be provided.
- Item return status analysis functionality will be provided.
Functions such as instant messaging, artificial intelligence-based photo recognition, a map of the university site and a mobile app will not be included within the MVP.

Priorities
High Priority
- Registration and sign-in functionality.
- Lost item reporting functionality.
- Found item documentation functionality.
- Information entry functionality (for example, item name, type) and photograph addition functionality.
- Item analysis functionality.
- Item linking analysis functionality.
  
Medium Priority
- Communications functionality.
- Report analysis functionality.
- Evidence of identification and ownership analysis documentation functionality.
- Item return/recovery status updating functionality.

Low Priority / Future Enhancements
- Instant messaging functionality for students and university members.
- Artificial intelligence-based photo recognition functionality.
- A university site map so that the location of lost and found items can be shown.
- A mobile app.
- Email and SMS communications functionality.
- Advanced system analytical functionality.
Constraints, Assumptions and Risks
The system will necessitate internet access and a suitable device. Moreover, it will depend upon university members documenting found items and analysing evidence of ownership.

Potential issues may include:
- Poor internet access.
- Insufficient resources to both build and then manage the system.
- Slow university member analysis/action.
- Incorrect claims in relation to found items.
- Inaccurate or incomplete information being entered by users.
Such issues can be minimised by appropriately managing the system, securing user accounts, effectively documenting information and correctly analysing evidence of ownership.

Further Queries
Before and/or during project development, the following queries will need to be analysed and then addressed:
- Will FindIt solely be developed for Babcock University or will it in future also be developed for other universities?
- Will students be required to register using their UMIS information?
- How will the system analyse evidence of ownership so that it can recognise the correct owner of an item?
- Which university department or members will manage the system?
- Should students be permitted to both amend and remove their reports?
- Should historical students’ lost items reports be automatically removed after a defined period?
- Should communications be provided via the system, via email and/or via SMS?
- How will the system manage inaccurate information and/or false reports?


9. EXPECTED OUTCOMES
It is anticipated that FindIt will:
- Provide students with an improved method of reporting lost items.
- Enable university members to more easily analyse found items.
- Facilitate better linking between students’ lost items reports and found items.
- Provide students with subsequent analysis and communications in relation to their reports.
- Enhance the likelihood that lost items are returned to their correct owners.
  
10. CONCLUSION
This project aims to further improve the effectiveness and structure of the procedure for students to report and for items to be returned at Babcock University.
By providing a centralised system for students to report lost items, for university members to document found items and to further enhance student-university member communication, the system is expected to effectively manage lost and found items.
Overall, it is anticipated that FindIt will further improve the analysis of lost items and so enhance the likelihood that lost items are returned to their correct owners.
